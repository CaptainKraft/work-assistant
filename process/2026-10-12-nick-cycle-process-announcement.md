# Nick's cycle process announcement

- **From:** Nick (founder), posted to the team Discord with @everyone
- **Received:** 2026-10-12
- **Full effect:** 2027-01-04 (next full cycle); partial rollout in the current cycle
- **Rollout:** EmberGen 2 first, then the other products

The post is reproduced verbatim below. The summary and checklists are in the root [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

---

@everyone

You may be wondering what's up with some of the changes that we're doing and requiring our Programmers to update us on what issues you're currently working on. Since the inception of JangaFX we've tried our best to have a flat management structure company and have let the team work amongst themselves to deliver features that I, a product lead (Morten, Gil, Javier), or an artist has suggested. Last year we implemented a new strategy to atleast get releases out every 2 months of whatever was ready and this has worked well. We will no longer have a flat management structure in the business. The past cycle was the last cycle we'll ever have without true, pre-planned direction. This flat structure leads to features filling an endless void of time in the name of perfection, not having concrete decisions taken by those working on the tasks and more.

JangaFX has one singular job: Provide incredible tools to professional artists and studios. Our entire mission is centered around improving the lives of the artists using our tools and giving them the features they want and need. To give artists these features, it costs money, and money unfortunately does not grow on trees. Our moat is ever shrinking with the power of AI tools and Generative AI features as is our monetary purse. We're also up against the likes of SideFX/Houdini, who consistently pump out industry shattering updates across many complex disciplines. Luckily, we also have the power of AI on our side to help us implement features faster, research faster, review PR's and more.

Many changes to the way our cyclces are planned and how our programmers work within them are going to come into effect over the next month or so and unfortunately we've missed the planning window for this particular cycle. Come January 4th, this plan will be in full effect for our next full cycle. In the meantime we will do our best to implement these structures in our current cycle.

What are the changes we're implementing?

Cooldown will no longer be a free-for all in tasks. Each cooldown period costs us approximately $141,000. We can no longer spend this money on free time if that's what you have used it for. This time period will be for fixing bugs in the backlog, helping teammates finish their tasks, any scope planning for larger tasks, and final QA before releases.

Management Hierarchy Nick -> Jeremiah (Product Manager) -> Product Leads (Morten (EG), Gil (IG), Javier(LG), Mihai (web)) -> Programming Team

Before a cycle starts, during cooldown the next cycle will be planned in full. Planning will take place in a few phases:

Phase 1: - What didn't get finished last cycle? Those items carry over. These items should be few, as we want to complete tasks within estimated time frames and cooldown will serve as the time to complete these items and fix bugs.

Phase 2: Artist feature call. Artists (Sebastien, Jason, Daniel, Shirk), Me, and Jeremiah will get on a call concerning each art driven product (EG/IG/LG). Feature requests, backed by actual artistic need to be voted on in order of importance to studios & customers.

Phase 3: The final aggregated list of tasks, along with assigned programmers will be shown to the product leads per product in individual calls. They can change personnel assignments, add new tasks, and triage through the artists list.

Phase 4: Dev estimations (Throughout the process).

Phase 5: Final overview call from Me and Jeremiah. We will work through any disagreements, and finalize the cycle based on all prior phases. Once this is greenlit, this is our set in stone cycle for the next 6 weeks. At this stage, every programmer will have things to do every day of the cycle.

What changes will the programmers have to abide by?

1. Because we will have specific tasks for every programmer throughout the entire cycle, we need to know the status of an item within a days level of accuracy. Every day we will have a written standup on, what you're doing and if you're on track. It is Jeremiah's job to make sure that the board is up to date based on what you have written. We would ask that if you finish a task, before moving on, you mark it as done and get the PR pushed through.

2. If there's a task that isn't on the board with allocated time, you aren't working on it without the approval of Jeremiah. Items being worked on need to be named and in our cycle milestone. If you find yourself on something that has no item, it is your responsibility to tell us. "Cycle breaking" items will always be run by Jeremiah before work is started. Any feature request is not a cycle breaking item. The only cycle breaking issues are serious bugs that completely halt a live studio customer from completing a production (imports/exports).

3. You are estimating timelines for your own work before we start the cycle so that we can track progress and know what team allocations are where.

4. If you are 150% past an estimation, stop working on that task. At that point we need to ship the task as is, re-scope it, or kill the feature. This decision will be recorded. Going overtime isn't a personal failure, but failing to report it is. We can't keep working on individual features for months.

5. Pull requests shouldn't sit for more than 24 hours. Mandatory peer-reviewed PR's are going away. YOU own this code and are responsible for the fallout. YOU will catch the flack from your teammates for bad code and are responsible for that. Claude will review your PR. Ensure that Claude reviews your PR, then send that thang.

6. Features come through intake, not you. The artist council votes on the order of importance. Technical items (debt, performance, crashes) go on the list through your product lead and get ranked. You don't build a feature because someone asked you directly, including me. Everyone's requests go through intake during cycle planning.

7. Mandatory meetings are mandatory. There will be weekly dev team meetings starting with EmberGen 2. These meetings are the time to hash out any issues you're facing, further scope items, and so on.

8. QA is your responsibility for your feature before and AFTER merging. If you need further testing, ask an artist. Regularly use PUP and potentially even a future Claude MCP to test your feature. Spending programmer time on QA'ing other tools is no longer a good use of our time. We will begin phasing out the QA teams in some capacity. More info TBD.

Overall, it is your job to work on the tasks in each cycle within the timeframe of that task. It is your job to tell us your status/progress each day so that the board is always current, as kept up by Jeremiah. It is your job to attend meetings pertaining to the product team you are on. If you are on a task, that means you own it. That means you decide how to implement it and do what needs to be done to ensure it is finished on time. If you need decision help, speak with your product lead. If you are the product lead, you are the end all be all on how something is done.

JangaFX needs to mature. JangaFX needs leadership, and a roadmap, and a path to success. We need to ensure our money is being spent towards features that customers and our artists genuinely need. For now we are implementing this process for EmberGen 2 and will slowly roll out the same process to the other products. EmberGen 2 is our priority right now due to it being our flagship product, and needing proper time allocations ASAP.

I trust that we can turn things around by implementing professional processes across the board. I would like for us to all feel proud for what we're shipping again.
